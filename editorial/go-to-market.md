# Commercial test for Design Notes

The product is a visual system design library for developers who want to explain a request, failure, and recovery. The free site proves the format with four complete systems. The 100-system catalog is a production target, not the first offer.

## First paid offer to test

Build **Reliability Volume 1** with 12 reviewed systems. Keep four complete lessons free. Add eight new systems around retries, outbox delivery, dead-letter recovery, webhooks, inventory reservation, order state, session management, and incident alerting. A paid system should include the interactive walkthrough, downloadable diagrams, a concise design guide, failure cases, and an interview practice prompt. Do not sell a promised volume until its contents and delivery are complete.

Test two price points, such as **$29 and $49**, with equal traffic and the same product. These are hypotheses, not evidence of willingness to pay. Show the full contents and sample before checkout. Start with one-time purchase; add a subscription only if new reviewed material ships on a schedule that justifies it.

## Demand test before ads

1. Publish short clips that show one failure and its recovery. Link each clip to the relevant free walkthrough.
2. Track qualified visits to a walkthrough, the share who reach its last step, guide opens, and clicks on a truthful paid-offer page.
3. Ask interested readers which system they need and what they would pay for. Record their answers separately from traffic counts.
4. Sell the completed first volume to a small audience. Measure completed purchases, refunds, and requests for the next volume. Do not infer revenue from clicks alone.
5. Buy ads only after the offer converts from unpaid traffic. Compare contribution margin per sale with acquisition cost; stop ads that lose money.

For illustration, a $39 sale through Gumroad's current direct-link fee schedule would incur a 10% platform fee plus $0.50 before any other applicable adjustments, leaving about $34.60. At a 2% purchase rate, a paid visit would need to cost materially less than $0.69 to leave room for support, refunds, and content costs. Both the conversion rate and visit cost are unverified assumptions. Check current fees before launch: https://gumroad.com/pricing

## Why this format may compete

System design courses and interview material already exist. ByteByteGo sells a broader system design learning product, so the offer needs a distinct benefit rather than another static note set: a reader can scroll through a request and see the active component, failure state, and recovery. Compare the actual experience and customer feedback with https://bytebytego.com/pricing before setting a final price.

## Production capacity

The shared renderer removes repeated frontend work. It does not remove research, architecture review, writing, or source checks. Release by reviewed volume, not by an arbitrary target date or a claim that all 100 are available. If a new system cannot pass the review gate in `production.md`, keep it planned.
